How to Rank on Apple Podcasts in 30 Days

Most podcasters publish more episodes and hope the Apple Podcasts algorithm notices. Ranking does not work that way. Category charts respond to ranking signals — consistent listens, audience behavior, and focused promotion — more than to episode count alone.

This guide explains what “ranking” means on Apple Podcasts, what you can influence in about 30 days, and how serious shows approach category movement without buying fake downloads.

What Apple Podcasts ranking actually means

When people say they want to rank, they usually mean category charts: Top 1–20 or Top 1–50 in Business, Health, Education, or another niche. Charts are competitive. Results depend on how crowded the category is and how consistent your release and listening patterns are.

No ethical podcast marketing service can guarantee a specific number. What you can do is strengthen the signals that correlate with chart movement.

Signals that matter more than “more content”

  • Steady, real listens in your target markets (often USA, UK, Canada)
  • Category fit — the right primary category for the show
  • Release consistency so the show stays active in the ecosystem
  • Discoverability outside the app (clips, cross-promotion, outreach) that brings intentional listeners

Spikes from low-quality traffic rarely hold. Charts punish patterns that look artificial.

A realistic 30-day ranking plan

Days 1–3: Confirm category, title, and show positioning. Fix anything that confuses new listeners.

Days 4–14: Run a focused promotion window aimed at ranking signals and download growth in your priority markets.

Days 15–30: Maintain momentum, watch category movement, and adjust. In many categories, early movement shows inside this window; competitive charts may need longer.

Common mistakes

  • Changing categories every week
  • Buying fake downloads to “force” a chart position
  • Expecting Top 1 in a hyper-competitive category in 7 days
  • Promoting with no clear audience geography
